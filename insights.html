Research note · Incident response

Incidents are sequences, not moments

The hardest part of an incident is often everything after it. Intake, triage, handoffs, evidence, after action review, corrective actions, and closure. Trust improves when this lifecycle is consistent and auditable — not just when the initial response is fast.

Most operational breakdowns happen not because a team failed to detect an incident, but because the process that follows detection is informal, ownership is unclear, and the evidence captured is insufficient for the scrutiny that comes later. Regulators and city partners do not only ask what happened. They ask what changed afterward.

Research note · Safety governance

UL 4600 is a safety argument that must be maintained

Organisations often treat UL 4600 compliance as a milestone rather than a continuous commitment. The friction appears not at initial certification but in maintenance — specifically in traceability between incidents and safety case updates, alignment between operational data and safety claims, and configuration changes across system revisions.

Safety maturity shows up in governance, not just in testing results. The question is not whether a system passed its initial safety case review. It is whether the safety case is still accurate after 50,000 miles of operational data have accumulated and three software updates have been deployed.

Research note · Regulatory relations

Trust is cumulative for cities and regulators

Stakeholders do not only ask what happened. They ask what changed afterward, how recurrence is prevented, and how consistency is maintained across markets. Evidence readiness and clear narratives make those conversations calmer — and those conversations are increasingly what determines whether an operator keeps its licence to operate in a given city.

The operators who build a track record of transparent, well-documented incident response are in a structurally different position when scrutiny arrives than those who are constructing their narrative after the fact. Trust is not built in a single incident response. It is built across dozens of them.

Research note · Deployment strategy

Start narrow, then scale

The instinct in large organisations is to design the complete end-to-end incident management system before deploying any of it. In practice, this creates a governance framework that is too abstract to implement and too rigid to adapt as operations evolve.

Organisations that make the most progress start with one workflow — incident triage, evidence packaging, or corrective action tracking — prove it works, then expand. Narrow implementation with genuine ownership is more valuable than a comprehensive process that exists only in documentation. The goal is a system that the operations team actually uses under pressure, not one that satisfies an audit on a calm day.
