"We're big believers in Rivian's approach — designing the vehicle, compute platform, and software stack together, while maintaining end-to-end control of scaled manufacturing and supply in the US."

Dara Khosrowshahi, CEO, Uber

Announced today — March 19, 2026 — this is Uber's most significant autonomous vehicle investment to date. An initial $300 million lands immediately, with four further tranches tied to Rivian hitting specific autonomy milestones through 2031. The full $1.25 billion only flows if Rivian delivers.

The first phase deploys 10,000 fully autonomous R2 SUVs in San Francisco and Miami in 2028. If milestones are met, Uber holds an option to purchase up to 40,000 additional vehicles starting 2030 — bringing the total fleet to 50,000. Every single vehicle will be exclusively available through Uber's app.

What makes this deal structurally different from Uber's other AV partnerships is that Uber is buying the vehicles itself — not just distributing another company's fleet. This marks a deliberate shift from pure marketplace to partial fleet ownership, taking on both asset and operational risk directly.

Rivian CEO RJ Scaringe has called autonomy the company's single biggest investment area. At SXSW 2026 last week, he said: "The rate of progress over the next five years will be so different that the past is not a good predictor of the future." The R2 consumer vehicle begins production this spring — the robotaxi variant follows.

Why this deal is different. Every previous Uber AV partnership kept Uber as a pure marketplace — the AV company owned and operated its vehicles, Uber just provided riders. With Rivian (and Lucid before it), Uber is buying the assets. That shifts the risk profile significantly — depreciation, utilisation rates, and incident liability now fall partly on Uber's balance sheet.

The exclusive clause is the real story. Rivian R2 robotaxis will be available exclusively through Uber. That is not just a distribution deal — it is a moat. No Lyft. No Waymo app. No direct consumer booking. Uber is not just the platform layer; it is becoming the only door through which a 50,000-vehicle fleet enters the market.

The milestone structure protects Uber. The $1.25B only fully flows if Rivian delivers on autonomy targets by specific dates. This is Uber funding Rivian's R&D while retaining the right to walk away if progress stalls. Smart capital deployment — or a sign that internal confidence in the timeline is measured.
