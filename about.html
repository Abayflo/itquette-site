About the Lab

Independent research.
Practical perspective.
No agenda.

ITquette Autonomous Lab was founded by Abiodun Olorunfemi on a straightforward observation: the autonomous vehicle industry is scaling faster than its operational infrastructure. The technology conversation is mature. The incident management, safety governance, and evidence readiness conversation is not.

Abiodun Olorunfemi brings a background in incident management and safety operations — the discipline of understanding how complex systems fail, how organisations respond under pressure, and what separates teams that improve consistently from those that repeat the same failure modes. That operational grounding shapes everything ITquette publishes.

Alongside that operational foundation, Abiodun has developed a research and intelligence practice focused specifically on the autonomous vehicle ecosystem — tracking partnerships, regulatory developments, technology moves, and deployment patterns across every layer of the AV stack. That breadth of intelligence informs the depth of the safety and incident work.

ITquette exists to bring those two threads together. Our work is grounded in how incident response and safety lifecycle management actually function in complex operational environments — not how they are described in standards documents or presented in conference papers.

We publish what we find openly. Our research is available through this site and our LinkedIn platform. When organisations find it relevant to something they are actively working through, we welcome that conversation — approached with genuine curiosity about the problem rather than a predetermined solution to sell.

What the research covers

How we think about this work

Operational over theoretical

Every insight is tested against what actually happens in complex operational environments — not what the standard says should happen or what a clean architecture diagram implies.

Specific over general

The AV industry has no shortage of general safety frameworks. What operators need is specific, applicable thinking about their particular scale, regulatory context, and operational structure.

Start narrow, build credibly

A complete governance framework that sits in a document and is never used is worse than nothing. We emphasise starting with one workflow, proving it works, then expanding — because that is how operational change actually happens.

Evidence as infrastructure

Good evidence readiness is not about preparing for the worst case incident. It is about building the operational infrastructure that makes every conversation with a regulator, city partner, or public stakeholder more grounded and more credible.

Trust is earned over time

No single incident response, however well handled, builds lasting trust. The operators who maintain their operating licences in competitive markets are the ones who have demonstrated consistent, improving behaviour across many incidents over time.

The ecosystem view matters

We track developments across AV technology companies, traditional automakers, ride hailing platforms, and regulators simultaneously. Patterns that are invisible within a single organisation become clear when viewed across the ecosystem.

Our research is relevant to anyone building, deploying, or regulating autonomous vehicle operations — AV technology companies, traditional automakers, ride hailing platforms, and city and national regulators. The incident management and safety governance challenges that matter are shared across all of them.
