The ITquette Thesis

Autonomous mobility is often discussed as a technology challenge. The conversation typically focuses on perception systems, autonomy stacks, simulation environments, edge cases, and vehicle performance.

These topics matter. But as autonomous systems move beyond testing and into real-world operations, a different challenge begins to emerge.

Organizations must do more than operate safely. They must demonstrate, explain, and continuously improve safety in ways that can be understood by operators, regulators, insurers, and the public.

ITquette explores how autonomous mobility organizations transform operational experience into credible evidence of safety improvement.

This research program examines how organizations learn from operational events, identify recurring patterns, implement corrective actions, measure improvement, and build confidence in their safety claims.

Research Notes

Shorter observations on incident response, safety governance, regulatory relations, and operational readiness in autonomous mobility.

Research note · Incident response

Incidents are sequences, not moments

The hardest part of an incident is often everything after it — triage, evidence collection, handoffs, after action review, and corrective action closure. Trust improves when this lifecycle is consistent and auditable, not just when the initial response is fast. Regulators and city partners do not only ask what happened. They ask what changed afterward.

Incident lifecycleEvidenceTrust
Research note · Safety governance

UL 4600 is a safety argument that must be maintained

Organisations often treat UL 4600 compliance as a milestone rather than a continuous commitment. Safety maturity shows up in governance, not just in testing results. The question is not whether a system passed its initial safety case review — it is whether the safety case remains accurate after 50,000 miles of operational data and three software updates.

Research note · Regulatory relations

Trust is cumulative for cities and regulators

Stakeholders ask what changed afterward, how recurrence is prevented, and how consistency is maintained across markets. The operators who build a track record of transparent, well-documented incident response are in a structurally different position when scrutiny arrives than those constructing their narrative after the fact.

Research note · Deployment strategy

Start narrow, then scale

The instinct in large organisations is to design the complete end-to-end governance system before deploying any of it. In practice this creates frameworks too abstract to implement and too rigid to adapt. Narrow implementation with genuine ownership is more valuable than a comprehensive process that exists only in documentation.
